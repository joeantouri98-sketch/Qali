# Changelog

Short notes on what changed in Qali (called LiNaK until November 2026). Newest first.

## Latest

### Added
- **Stark map stage** (Atomic response > **Stark map**; `qali --pipeline stark Rb --state 50s1/2`). A Rydberg state of Li–Fr in a static field, with the engine below:
  - writes `<El>_stark.json` and the page `plots/<El>/<El>_stark.html`: every eigenvalue near the state against field, coloured by its overlap with the state (character), the followed state, −½αF², the avoided crossings it meets (field and gap), and the field-free levels in the window; a field cursor reads off shift, character and the nearest other level; GHz, MHz or cm⁻¹; CSV export;
  - polarisability for every |m_j|, with scalar and tensor parts for j ≥ 3/2 (MHz/(V/cm)² and a.u.);
  - defaults: basis n ± 4 with every l, fields up to the Inglis–Teller field 1/(3n*⁵) a.u., energies within ±0.6 manifold spacings. Rb 50S (891 states, 300 fields) takes about 40 s;
  - Figures: **Stark map (Rydberg state in a static or AC field)**, the same map with the manifolds grey under the coloured states;
  - against ARC's Stark maps: polarisabilities of Cs 60S, Na 40S, K 40S, Li 40S within 0.07 %, Rb and Cs 50D₅/₂ (m_j = ½) within 0.3 and 0.7 %; Rb 50S first avoided crossing at 2.079 V/cm (O'Sullivan & Stoicheff: 2.08). Cs 8S and 9S polarisabilities are 1.2 % and 2.3 % above the measured 9.490(12) MHz/(kV/cm)² and 150 700(1100) a.u.: below n ≈ 10 the model-potential dipoles are the limit.
- **Pair potential stage** (Atomic response > **Pair potential**; `qali --pipeline pair Rb --state 60s1/2`). Two Rydberg atoms of Li–Fr, with the engine `qali.physics.rydberg_stark.pair`:
  - dipole-dipole V = [d1·d2 − 3(d1·n)(d2·n)]/R³ at any axis angle θ, over pair states within an energy cut of the chosen pair, keeping (−1)^(l1+l2) and, at θ = 0, M; diagonalised in the exchange-symmetric and antisymmetric blocks separately (the method of ARC's PairStateInteractions);
  - each eigenvalue coloured by its weight on the chosen pair and its swap;
  - C6 from second order in the pair's degenerate manifold (all m_j, both orders), with the chosen pair's value, the manifold's eigenvalues and the channels with their Förster defects and shares; sign ΔE = C6/R⁶ (repulsive > 0; ARC quotes −C6); every pair the basis gives by default, `--c6-cut` for ARC's 25 GHz;
  - C3 when the two levels are dipole coupled (nS + nP), from the first-order matrix, for the chosen pair's eigenstates;
  - blockade radius for a Rabi frequency (default Ω/2π = 1 MHz), from C6 (or C3) and from the diagonalised potential; LeRoy radius;
  - defaults: n ± 4, l up to the pair's l + 3, pair states within 1.7 manifold spacings (converged: the 60S shift at 2.5–4 µm moves by < 0.1 % up to 250 GHz), R from 0.3 to 2 blockade radii;
  - page `plots/<El>/<El>_pair.html` (potentials, C6/R⁶ or ±C3/R³, blockade radius and ±Ω, distance cursor, C6 channels and manifold tables, CSV) and figure **Pair potential (two Rydberg atoms)**;
  - against ARC: Rb 60S C6 138.87 vs 138.88 GHz µm⁶ (25 GHz cut; manifold eigenvalues within 0.02 %), Rb 62D₃/₂ 766.96 vs 767.03, Cs 60S 101.60 vs 101.61, Rb 60D₅/₂ m_j = 5/2 at θ = 0, π/4, π/2 within 0.01 %; Rb 60S pair potential in ARC's basis (1617 pair states) at 2.5–8 µm within 0.02 %. Rb 60S, all pairs: C6 = 137.77 GHz µm⁶, R_b = 7.19 µm at 1 MHz.
- **AC Stark maps** (Stark map stage, **AC frequency**; `qali --pipeline stark Rb --state 56d5/2 --ac-frequency 2 --field-max 3 --n-range 6 --l-max 10`). The field F_dc + F_ac cos(2πft), solved with Shirley's time-independent Floquet Hamiltonian (Phys. Rev. 138, B979 (1965)), the method of ARC's `ShirleyMethod`:
  - photon blocks q = −Q…Q (**AC sidebands**, default 2) with H0 + F_dc z + q·hf on the diagonal and (F_ac/2) z between neighbours, so F_ac is the peak amplitude, as in ARC;
  - without a static field the matrix splits by (−1)^(l+q), a quarter of the work;
  - the map is quasienergy against F_ac, coloured by the weight of the state with no photons added; default window ±1.5 f;
  - also the dynamic polarisability α(ω) for every |m_j| (AC shift −¼α(ω)F_ac²), and a truncation check: the change from one more sideband at the largest amplitude where the state is still clean;
  - against ARC's ShirleyMethod: Rb 56D₅/₂ at 2 GHz (Q = 2) and Cs 40S at 5 GHz (Q = 3), same bases, target shifts within 0.05 % up to 3 V/cm, including the strongly mixed range near the Inglis–Teller field;
  - the page and the Stark map figure show the AC frequency, sidebands, α(ω) and the −¼α(ω)F² curve.
- **Rydberg Stark engine** (`qali.physics.rydberg_stark`). Stark maps by exact diagonalisation of H0 + F z in a |n l j m_j⟩ basis at one m_j, the method of ARC (Šibalić et al. 2017):
  - **Energies, fine-structure resolved** (`defects.py`). Quantum defects from precision spectroscopy, with the ionization energy each set was fitted with and the isotope's reduced-mass Rydberg constant: Li-7 (Goy 1986; Lorenzen & Niemax 1983), Na-23 (Lorenzen & Niemax 1983), K-39 (Peper et al. 2019), Rb-85 (Li et al. 2003; Han 2006; Moore 2020), Cs-133 (Deiglmayr et al. 2016; Lorenzen & Niemax 1984; Weber & Sansonetti 1987). NIST levels below each table's lowest n; Fr and any series the table lacks: the NIST levels, and a δ0 + δ2/(n−δ0)² fit to them above; higher l: the core-polarisation defect (Rb g: 0.0039 against 0.0040 measured). New table **Rydberg quantum defects (j-resolved) and ionization energy** in Preferences > Tables and variables.
  - Against NIST: K, Na and Cs within 0.03 cm⁻¹ over the series; Rb np 0.13 cm⁻¹ above n = 10, where NIST's Rb np levels are the weak side (identical p₁/₂ and p₃/₂ energies above 27p); Li's NIST levels scatter by ±0.5 cm⁻¹ (up to 3 cm⁻¹ at 11p) around the millimetre-wave defects.
  - **Radial integrals** (`radial.py`): Numerov on a √r grid in the Marinescu potential with spin–orbit, at each level's own energy, all states of one l, j integrated together. Hydrogen ⟨n l|r|n l+1⟩ exact to 10⁻⁷.
  - **Checks against measurements, Rb**: 9S and 10S polarisabilities 103.95 and 272.62 MHz/(kV/cm)² against 103.77(9) and 272.54(16); 8D₅/₂ α0 221.6 / α2 54.2 against 222.68(14) / 51.91(10), 8D₃/₂ α0 229.4 against 230.68(25); nS from 15 to 80 within 0.7 % of O'Sullivan & Stoicheff's fit (PRA 31, 2718 (1985)); the first avoided crossing of 30S and 50S within 0.1 and 0.6 % of theirs.
- **Third-order Brueckner dipoles: structural radiation and normalisation** (`qali.physics.atomic_structure.dhf_sr`). The Brueckner stage's E1 matrix elements now include the two third-order pieces it left out: structural radiation (the photon acting inside the correlation potential Σ₂, i.e. Σ₂ differentiated with respect to the field; every internal line takes one insertion, and the core's RPA response replaces or rotates core orbitals) and normalisation (the weight the valence orbital loses to the correlated part of the state, from dΣ₂/dE). Both are scaled by the same λ as Σ₂. They matter most where the radial integral cancels: the weak ground ns → np lines.
  - Precision checks (n-max 25): D lines K +0.06 / +0.03 %, Rb +0.02 / −0.04 %, Cs −0.15 / −0.15 %, Fr +0.02 / −0.20 %, Na +0.17 %. Weak lines: K 4s → 5p +0.04 %, Rb 5s → 6p −0.13 / +0.42 %, Cs 6s → 7p −0.55 / −0.44 % (before: −3 to −7 %); Cs 7s → 6p −0.13 / −0.11 % (before +0.5 %).
  - Ground ns → np beyond the first lines: the spread of Brueckner against the measured f-values drops from 26 → 5 % (Rb np₃/₂), 54 → 13 % (Rb np₁/₂), 77 → 10 % (Cs np₁/₂), 22 → 6 % (K np₁/₂). Cs np₁/₂ loses its spurious Cooper minimum (measured/Brueckner was up to 190 at 13p; now 0.7–1.1 to 21p). Beyond the measured lines Brueckner now agrees with the Extrapolator for Rb and Fr (Extrapolator/Brueckner np₃/₂ 0.99 and 1.03, np₁/₂ 1.15 and 1.21; no line beyond 2σ, where Fr had 16).
  - α₀ moves by at most 0.2 %: K 291.31 → 290.76, Rb 319.04 → 318.67, Cs 399.94 → 399.95, Fr 315.42 → 315.94 a.u.
  - Checks: inserting the unit operator gives SR + N = 0 exactly (particle number); ⟨w|Σ|w⟩ from the same sums equals the Σ₂ matrices; ⟨w‖SR‖v⟩ and ⟨v‖SR‖w⟩ agree. The angular factors are explicit sums over magnetic substates, so no 6j algebra is hand-coded. Against Roberts, Fairhall & Ginges, PRA 107, 052812 (2023): weak lines Cs 6s → 7p +7.0 / +4.3 % (theirs +6.8 / +4.2 %); on the D lines SR and N nearly cancel (−0.01 %, theirs −0.16 %).
  - Cost: minutes per species; the whole stage at `--n-max 25` on two cores takes Na 3, K 6, Rb 12, Cs 18, Fr 32 min. **Third-order dipoles** in the stage (`--no-third-order` to leave it out). Brueckner stage revision 2.
- **Extrapolator vs DHF / Brueckner** (Figures: "Extrapolator vs DHF / Brueckner (ns→np f)"; `qali.physics.extrapolation.crosscheck`). Ground ns → np_J f-values as f·n*³ against n, one panel per J: measured lines, NIST rows from spliced or extrapolated sources (shown apart), the Extrapolator with its 68 % band and its model without the fit, and the DHF and Brueckner stage outputs. Each model gets an uncertainty from its scatter against the measured lines, widened line by line where its radial integral cancels (1 %/c for a cancellation c = |net|/Σ|parts|; Cs 6s → 12p₁/₂ cancels to 0.8 %), and each line beyond the measured ones a pull against the Extrapolator; **Data...** lists them. Run the DHF / Brueckner stages with `--n-max 25` or so to reach beyond NIST. Findings, n ≤ 25:
  - np₃/₂: DHF and Brueckner (with its third-order dipoles, above) agree with the measured lines and with the Extrapolator beyond them: Rb and Fr within 1–7 %, Na and K 20–26 % low (within 2σ). Cs is measured to 25p.
  - np₁/₂: the radial integral cancels to 1–5 % above n ≈ 10, and to below 1 % for Cs. DHF puts a Cooper minimum near n ≈ 21 in Cs, which the measured lines (to 21p) do not show, and runs 1.4–9× below the Extrapolator for Fr; Brueckner follows the measured lines (Cs 0.7–1.1) and sits 15–21 % below the Extrapolator for Rb and Fr. Beyond 21p the Extrapolator falls 2–3× below Brueckner (22p₁/₂–25p₁/₂ beyond 2σ): nothing measured decides it.
  - Without the third order Brueckner sat 10–40 % low (its λ-scaled Σ₂ pulls the dipoles low; the third order restores them).
  - Na 11p–18p and K 10p–13p in NIST come from extrapolated sources; DHF, Brueckner and the Extrapolator all put the Na ones about 2× lower.
- **Model levels in the Rydberg stage.** **Model levels** (`--model-levels dhf|brueckner`, `--model-file`) fills the series NIST has no level of from the DHF or Brueckner stage output: the model's levels J by J up to its highest n (Brueckner: n_ground + 6), and above that the series continues with a Ritz fit δ0 + δ2/(n − δ0)² to them. The levels are marked theoretical, take the model's name as their source, and carry an uncertainty from the model's misfit to the NIST levels it was not fitted to, scaled as 1/n*³. Series with measured levels keep their quantum-defect fit (one measured level and the Ritz law beat a calculation). For the alkalis this is the Fr nf series (5f to 13f, δ_f ≈ 1.03); Li to Cs have NIST levels in every series the models cover.
  - Checked by taking the f series of Na to Cs as if unmeasured: the Brueckner f levels are within 0.2 cm⁻¹ of NIST (DHF 0.5 cm⁻¹), against 14–76 cm⁻¹ for a hydrogenic series, and within the stated uncertainty. Li 5f is the exception: Brueckner, DHF and a hydrogenic level all sit 7 cm⁻¹ from the NIST value.
  - With Brueckner f levels, Fr nd (n ≥ 8) lifetimes drop 3–6 % (decay to 5f, 6f); 5f₅/₂ lives 47.9 ns, 5f₇/₂ 50.0 ns. α₀ and the 7s–7p₁/₂ magic wavelengths do not change (no f channel).
  - Grotrian: model levels dashed (figure) or in their own style (Grotrian page), with a note.

### Fixed
- **Fr nf series.** A series with no NIST level was hydrogenic (δ = 0) from the ground state's n up: Fr "6f" sat where 7f lies (n* = 6 against 4.97), every f level was labelled one n too low, and 5f, the lowest member, was missing. Such a series now starts above the core's shells of its l and takes their number as the integer part of its quantum defect (Fr: one f shell, 4f, so δ_f = 1, series from 5f); its fractional part needs a model (above). Fr 5g is now generated too. The Rydberg stage revision is 2; Li to Cs are unchanged.

## 3.1.0 – 2026-11-18

### Added
- **Every magic wavelength, and what limits each one.** The Polarizability stage now keeps every crossing of α_ground and α_excited. Before, it dropped those within a fixed ~330 GHz band of any line, a cut tied to nothing physical (it kept Rb 492.16 nm and dropped 506.97 nm, which is less sensitive). Next to nearly every excited-state line there is one: Rb 92 between 474 and 2000 nm, Cs 88. Each now carries, from the line widths of the Lifetimes stage (each line's own A when it hasn't run):
  - **heat-out time**: how long photon recoil takes to heat atoms out of the trap, t = 3ħ|Re α|/(4 E_rec Im α) (Grimm et al. 2000), the same at any trap depth; and the ground state's photons/s in a 1 mK deep trap;
  - **magic window**: the laser-frequency range over which |α_ground − α_excited| stays within 1 % of α (Rb: 0.1–0.5 GHz next to the Rydberg lines, 4 GHz at 1351 nm);
  - the trap photons the excited state absorbs per natural decay (1 mK deep).
  **Practical** by default means only a heat-out time of at least 1 s (`--magic-heating-min`); that rules out the crossings next to ground-state lines (Na 589.46 nm, between the D lines: 0.08 s) and nothing else, since every other crossing is usable. Rb 787.6 nm takes 12 s. Optional further limits: `--magic-window-min` (GHz) and `--magic-excite-max`.
  - There is about one crossing next to every excited-state line, so most of them sit by the Rydberg lines piling up toward the excited state's ionization limit (Rb: 76 below 500 nm), and their number grows with the Rydberg stage's n_max. Each records the line it sits by (`line_label`, `line_n`). Listed and drawn by default: those by lines with n = 1 to n_ground + 5 (Li 7, Na 8, K 9, Rb 10, Cs 11, Fr 12), which keeps every one in the published tables (Arora et al. 2007; Safronova et al. 2012; Singh et al. 2016) and leaves out the pile-up: Rb 12 of 92. `--magic-n N_MIN N_MAX`, **Lines n from, to** in the figure and on the Polarizability page; every crossing is still saved.
  - α(λ) figure, **Crossings**: *Cut near lines, full in a window* (the default; the curves are cut within ± a set width of each line, 0.5 nm, except in a window you sweep across the spectrum), *All crossings*, and *Practical only* (curves hidden where the heat-out time is too short, and around crossings outside the limits you set, so every visible crossing is marked). **Samples** sets the points across the range; the magic markers have their own type, fill, size and colour.
  - Polarizability page: **Practical only** in the magic-wavelength filters (on by default), and the window and heat-out time in the table.
- **DHF and Brueckner continuum.** With DHF or Brueckner f-values (`--model-f`), the ground level's photoionization continuum in Polarizability (and so Tweezer and Blackbody) now comes from the same model, not the calibrated model potential: the positive-energy states of the model's basis cavity (r ≤ 3000 a₀), a discretized continuum whose pseudo-lines are a quadrature of df/dε (about 430 per species). `<El>_dhf.json` and `<El>_brueckner.json` gain a `continuum` list of (ω, f) in a.u.; Brueckner's dipoles carry the RPA dressing (at most at the highest bound level's frequency). `<El>_polarizability.json` records `continuum_model`. Other levels keep the calibrated model's continuum.
  - Threshold cross-sections (0.05 eV smoothing): Rb 0.086 Mb (DHF), 0.065 Mb (Brueckner), measured 0.076(15); Na 0.079 / 0.078 Mb, measured 0.117(15); K 0.0067 Mb (DHF), measured 0.010(2.5). The Rb Cooper minimum falls at 0.6–0.7 eV above the limit, where it is observed.
  - The cavity resolves the continuum well up to about 1 eV above the limit; above that the pseudo-states are sparse (fine for α and C₆, too coarse for a df/dε curve). The DHF dipole keeps its static core-polarization correction at all energies, so its continuum oscillator strength (Rb 0.056, Cs 0.068) is larger than the calibrated model's (0.024, 0.022), mostly from 10–100 eV; α₀ moves by at most 0.05 a.u. (Rb 318.80 → 318.84, Cs 399.79 → 399.84), C₆ by about 0.1 %.
- **Version stamps on stage outputs.** Every JSON a stage writes starts with a `"_qali"` block, and every HTML page carries it in a `<meta name="qali-stamp">` tag: Qali version, stage, the stage's output revision, the time it was made (UTC) and the command. `qali.core.stamps.STAGE_REVISIONS` holds each stage's revision; bump it whenever a stage's outputs change in a way that makes older files wrong.
  - The Plots, Data and Shared lists mark outputs: ⚠ before an outdated one (made by an older revision of its stage, or built from a stage output that has been rerun since). The tooltip says why, or which Qali made the file; one without a stamp (made by a Qali before 2026-11-17 or edited by hand) says so in its tooltip and in Properties. Bundled data still exactly as seeded counts as current.
- **Right-click > Properties...** on any file in the Plots, Data and Shared lists: name, folder, size, created and modified dates, the version stamp (Qali version, stage, revision, when, command), whether it is current and why not, and a few summary fields (species, n_states, method, inputs, ...).
- **Faster tests.** `pytest-xdist` joins the dev dependencies. **Run > Tests** gets **Parallel** (on by default when xdist is installed: all cores) and **Skip slow** (leaves out the 16 tests that run whole DHF / Brueckner calculations, marked `slow`; from the command line `-m "not slow"`). On two cores the suite takes 1 min 17 s parallel without the slow tests, against about 5.5 min before.
- **Blackbody figure: map over n and temperature.** τ_eff, Γ_BBR or Γ_BBR/Γ_rad of one series (s, p₁/₂, d₃/₂, ...) on an (n, T) grid, computed with the Blackbody stage's bound-bound physics on the Lifetimes network; it matches the stage's own temperature sweep exactly. For Γ_BBR/Γ_rad a white line marks where the blackbody rate equals the radiative rate (Rb np at 300 K: n ≈ 32). The colour range spans 6 decades below the maximum. The temperature-sweep view can show Γ_BBR/Γ_rad too.
- **Publication figures.** A **Figures** tab in the viewer and in the bottom panel draws figures with matplotlib from the current results. matplotlib is now a required dependency (`uv sync` installs it).
  - Figures: Grotrian diagram, quantum defects δ(n), scaling laws τ, τ_eff and |α₀| against n* with power-law fits, theory vs experiment (α₀, C₆, D-line lifetimes, % deviation per species), and α(λ) for the ground and first p levels with magic wavelengths.
  - Grotrian: **Levels and lines...** picks the levels (grouped by l, with quick selection by n and l), then the lines between them, listed by A with λ, f and the f source. J columns, wavelength labels along the lines, arrows, colour and line width (∝ log A) are options. Labels of near-degenerate levels are spread apart. Wavelength labels with no room on their line are left off, and a note says so.
  - Quantum defects: dashed Ritz fits δ₀ + δ₂/n*² + δ₄/n*⁴ for each J series (weighted by 1/n*³, lowest member left out for series of 7 or more). Against published δ₀ for Li to Cs the median difference is 1×10⁻⁴. The Rydberg stage's single fit per l, which mixes the two J series, is no longer drawn.
  - α₀ and C₆ by line, from the Polarizability stage's own sum (core and continuum included, so the parts add up to its α₀ and C₆): the contribution of each line, the partial sum against n_max with the ±σ band, and the uncertainty budget (each line's share of σ², by first-order propagation as in the stage). For Rb 5s, 5s–5p carries 96.7 % of α₀ and 99.1 % of σ², and the core 2.85 % of α₀.
  - Decay branching of any level: branching ratios with wavelengths, from the Lifetimes stage.
  - Decay cascade from any level to the ground state: the share of atoms passing through each level, arrows as wide as the photons per atom on each line, and the mean time to reach the ground state, T = τ + Σ BR·T(lower). Radiative decay only (0 K). Every drawn line carries its wavelength. **Data...** lists the photons per atom on every line.
  - df/dε of the ground state across the ionization limit: f·n*³ of the bound lines, J by J (measured filled, model open), and the photoionization continuum of the Polarizability stage. df/dε is continuous at the limit, so a step marks f-values that disagree. Na meets the continuum within 2 % and shows its Cooper minimum about 1.4 eV above the limit.
  - Blackbody: dynamic BBR shifts of Rydberg levels against n (they level off near +2.4 kHz at 300 K), Γ_rad, Γ_BBR and Γ_ion against n, and the temperature sweep as τ_eff(T) = 1/(Γ_rad + Γ_BBR(T)) or Γ_BBR(T).
  - Breit–Rabi diagram of any level with hyperfine constants, diagonalized exactly (dipole, quadrupole, octupole).
  - Feshbach a(B) for one or more entrance channels, with the poles marked and the curve broken across them.
  - Radial wavefunctions u(r) or r²|R|² from the same model potential as the orbital pages, with the ionic core shaded, ⟨r⟩, nodes and the fraction inside the core.
  - Plot builder: any list in a stage output, or in any JSON file, is a table. x, y, error bars and filters are columns or expressions (`log10`, `sqrt`, comparisons, `&`, `|`, `col("name")`); nothing else is evaluated. Several series, also across species, with markers, lines, error bars, ±error bands, steps or bars. A lower panel can show the other series against series 1 (difference, ratio or %).
  - Plot builder markers: each series sets its marker type (circle, square, triangles, diamond, pentagon, hexagon, star, plus, cross, point), filled or open, and its own size (auto: the size under Size and style).
  - Plot builder elements: a series can go on a right-hand y axis (own label and scale, one legend for both) or in an inset (own ranges, with the zoomed region marked). Heat maps and contours of a z column, on the grid when the points form one, else triangulated, with a colour bar. A broken x or y axis leaves out a range; each side spans its own data. A broken axis can't be combined with a right-hand axis or an inset, and is not zoomed with the mouse. The exported script draws all of these the same way.
  - Spectrum: absorption (cross-section, optical depth or transmission of a vapour cell) and emission spectra from the Spectrum stage's physics (Boltzmann populations, Voigt lines), and the D lines with the hyperfine components of every isotope. With no vapour data (Fr), the cross-section per atom.
  - Trap depth and scattering rate against wavelength from the Tweezer stage's physics: U in µK (symlog) over R_sc, with the strongest lines marked.
  - Size and style: APS, Nature, thesis or slides, one or two columns, or a size in cm. Palettes Okabe–Ito, Tol and grey. Computer Modern, STIX or sans fonts, or LaTeX (needs `latex`, `dvipng` for PNG/TIFF and the cm-super fonts). Ticks, grid, line and marker size, and panel labels (a), (b). **Use as default** keeps the style for new figures.
  - **Preferences > Plots**: the size, style, export format and resolution new figures start with (shared with **Use as default**).
  - Axes: labels, title, linear, log or symlog scales, limits and legend.
  - Input: each figure reads the usual output of its stages, a run saved under a name with Save as (`<El>_<stage>__<name>.json`), or another file of that stage, chosen per stage. The plot builder lists named runs as tables of their own. The figure's note names a chosen input.
  - Zoom and pan on the figure: mouse wheel (Shift: x only, Alt: y only), drag, right-drag to a box, double-click for the full view. They set the axis ranges, so the export, the script and the recipe show the same view. Text and arrows are clipped to the panel when the range is set. The controls are listed in Preferences > Plots.
  - Export PDF, SVG, EPS, PNG or TIFF (600 dpi, or as set in Preferences > Plots). Fonts are embedded as TrueType, and TIFF is LZW-compressed. **Script...** writes a standalone numpy + matplotlib script, **Data...** the numbers as CSV (levels and lines for a Grotrian), and **Save...** a `.qfig` recipe.
  - `qali --pipeline figure recipe.qfig [--out file] [--species El] [--input stage=run|file] [--script file.py]` redraws a recipe from the current results.
- **Plot windows.** A ↗ button beside the viewer's tabs and beside the bottom panel's ⇅ opens that panel's plot in a window of its own. Each press opens a new window, so several stay open side by side. An interactive page gets its own copy, a figure is a snapshot as it was, and the JSON viewer a copy of its text.
- **Whole-view zoom.** Ctrl + wheel (or Ctrl + = / − / 0) magnifies the whole plot in the viewer, in the Figures tab and in plot windows; middle-drag or the scroll bars move a magnified view. In a figure window the plain wheel zooms too, dragging moves the view and a double-click fits it. The Figures tab's y-only data zoom moves from Ctrl + wheel to Alt + wheel.
- **Advanced > Export** leaves out the Figures tab's last folder, a path on this computer.
- **⁸⁵Rb Feshbach resonances.** Run Feshbach for Rb with isotope 85 to get ⁸⁵Rb's own resonances, mass and scattering lengths. Before, the stage changed only the mass and warned that the ⁸⁷Rb resonances were used.
  - `Rb_feshbach.json` gains an `"isotopes"` block. The (2,−2)+(2,−2) and (2,+2)+(2,+2) resonances are those observed by Blackley et al., PRA 87, 033611 (2013), with their coupled-channel widths and background scattering lengths (−380 to −474 a₀); all s-wave rows and d-wave rows with |Δ| ≥ 0.2 mG are included, as for ⁸⁷Rb. - **Feshbach: d- and g-wave lines left out of a(B).** "d-wave" names the closed-channel molecular state; with an s-wave entrance channel it is still a (narrow) pole of the s-wave scattering length, as are g-wave states. They were drawn as markers only. Even-ℓ resonances now enter a(B); odd-ℓ (p, f) ones still do not.
- **Feshbach: ⁶Li zero crossings.** The local fits of Zürn et al. put the |1⟩ + |2⟩ zero at 569.9 G; the measured one is at 527.5 G. ⁶Li now uses their coupled-channel table (zero at 527.3 G).
- **Model f-value uncertainty ignored the Preferences table.** Preferences > Tables and variables > Uncertainty model has DHF and Brueckner rows; Polarizability used them, but Lifetimes gave model lines a fixed 5%. Lifetimes now reads the same table (default still 5%).
- **Spectrum line heights.** Both old spectrum pages scaled every line's peak to its f whatever its width: a stick plot drawn with widths. Physically a line's area is π r_e c f, so a Doppler peak scales as f·λ and a natural one as f/Γ; UV against IR lines and broad against narrow lines were wrong. The broadenings were also shown one at a time instead of as one Voigt profile. The Spectra stage's "absorption" bar used every line in the table, excited lower levels included, as if every level were populated, and its "emission" bar was weighted by the absorption f instead of N_u A hν. Lines with ΔE at the table's 1e-6 eV rounding step (Rb had a "line" at 1.24 m) are now left out. All fixed in the new Spectrum page below.
- **Stages crawled when the CPU was shared.** numpy's BLAS started 4–6 threads per stage on a 20-core Windows PC, which gave no speed-up on LiNaK's few-hundred-row matrices (DHF Na: 31 s on the default threads, 26 s on one). Under a full test run those spinning threads slowed the fitted DHF runs past the tests' 15-minute timeout (25–40 s alone). LiNaK now sets one BLAS thread per process (`OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS`, `MKL_NUM_THREADS`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS`) unless you set them yourself; results are unchanged.
- **Breit–Rabi curves swapped levels at every crossing.** All eigenvalues were sorted together at each field and curve k kept the zero-field label of the k-th lowest level, so wherever two levels of different m_F crossed (22 times below 100 G for Na 3p3/2, throughout the Paschen–Back region of every excited state) the colour and the |F, m_F⟩ label jumped to another level. The Hamiltonian is now solved block by block in m_F, which is exact at every field: each curve is one level and keeps its labels.
- **g_J used g_S = 2.** The Landé formula took the electron spin g factor as 2, so ground-state g_J was 2.0000 instead of ≈2.0023 and every Zeeman slope was 0.1% small (3p3/2: 1.3333 vs measured 1.3341). It now uses g_S = 2.00231930436 and the reduced-mass g_L = 1 − m_e/M, and the bundled constants give the measured ground-state g_J (Na 2.0022960, K 2.002295, Rb 2.00233113, Cs 2.002542; Steck, Vanden Bout et al. Phys. Rev. 165, 88 (1968)).
- **g_I ignored diamagnetic shielding.** The nuclear Zeeman term used the bare μ_I/I from the IAEA table. Inside the atom the electron cloud shields the nucleus (0.4% for Rb, 0.6% for Cs), which moves every field-insensitive point: the 87Rb |1,−1⟩ ↔ |2,+1⟩ magic field came out at 3.2418 G instead of the measured 3.228917 G. The constants files now carry the measured atomic g_I′ (Arimondo, Inguscio & Violino 1977, `_gI_prime`) for Na, 39K, 41K, 85Rb, 87Rb and Cs, used when present; the stage prints which g_I it used. If your data folder has older copies of `<El>_hf_constants.json`, the stage says so: delete them to pick up the measured values.
- **Hyperfine level diagram:** "E1" connectors were drawn between any two consecutive states, including 3p1/2 → 3p3/2 (same parity, not an E1 line); the Zeeman page printed half-integer F and m_F rounded (210Fr, I = 6, showed F = 6 for 11/2).
- **Merged-J levels in the transitions list:** NIST lists the f and g levels from n = 12 as one level (`5/2,7/2`, `7/2,9/2`), and the transitions stage tested the ΔJ rule with the first J only, dropping f → g9/2 lines (for Na, 18 lines between f n = 12 to 20 and 18g9/2 or 6g9/2). Every J of a merged level is now tested. Only Na was affected (Li, K, Rb, Cs and Fr rebuild to identical lists). Na's bundled transitions and lifetimes files are regenerated: the 18g9/2 lifetime drops 12% (it lacked decay channels its 18g7/2 partner had), the f5/2 lifetimes n = 12 to 20 change by under 0.2%. Rerun Transitions, then Lifetimes, Polarizability and Blackbody for your own species data.

### Interface
- **More tables in Preferences > Tables and variables:** isotope masses (Species constants); the M2+ core polarizabilities and the NIST Handbook limits and input levels of the one-valence ions (new group Structure models); the D-line isotope shifts, the vapour-pressure coefficients and the default cell temperatures (new group Spectrum). Rows can be added to the isotope masses and cell temperatures. The ion levels and vapour coefficients are read when a stage runs, so a saved edit applies to the next run.
- **Pages in the app follow its theme.** A page opened in the app's viewer switches to the app's light or dark theme, and switches again when you change the app's theme; its own Theme button still works. In a browser the pages follow the system as before.
- **Spectrum page, Strip tab:** hovering a line in either strip shows it (levels, air and vacuum wavelength, f, A, source, relative strength); clicking picks it for the curve of growth.
- **Highest n, not an offset.** The DHF + core polarisation and Brueckner stages take `--n-max` ("Highest n" in the GUI), the highest principal quantum number to report, instead of `--n-extra`, an offset from the valence n. Blank keeps the old default, valence n + 6 (Na 9, Cs 12). `--n-extra` now stops with the equivalent `--n-max` (for Na, `--n-extra 27` is `--n-max 30`).
- **Old Polarizability and Optical tweezer pages retired.** The Polarizability page (Visualise) has shown them since its round; the command line could still write `<El>_polarizability.html`, `<El>_magic_wavelengths.html` and `<El>_tweezer.html`, and the bundled copies were still shipped. Both stages now write data only (Polarizability still writes the cross-element C6 matrix and α(0) validation with `--all`; its `--no-plot` skips those). Delete the three files from your plots folder.
- **Grotrian diagram without ORCA.** The stage no longer stops when there is no ORCA data: the levels and lines come from the Rydberg, Transitions and Lifetimes stages, and the TD-DFT / SOC levels are drawn over them only when present. It stops only when there are no levels at all.
- **Optical tweezer moved up** in Atomic response, right after Polarizability: it is the stage that uses α(ω) directly. Blackbody now comes third. Neither stage needs the other, so nothing about running them changes.
- Options of the DHF + core polarisation and Brueckner stages are grouped into Model, Fit to NIST, Input and Output; the fit options are now ticks that say what they do ("Fit p levels", "Fit d levels", "Second d level", "Scale Σ2 to NIST") instead of "No ..." ticks. Stage options can name their section (`section=` in the stage table).
- Stage options are grouped into Input, Output and Options sections, with path pickers and grey default hints.
- Compare, transitions and lifetimes inputs reworked; combo boxes open downward.
- Extrapolation dialog: wider plot by default, the example in Edit datasets is now a template you can copy, unreliable rows are shown unticked and disabled.
- Shorter hover texts everywhere.
